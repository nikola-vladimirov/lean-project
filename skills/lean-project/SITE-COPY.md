# Site copy

Write website text that is accurate, specific and sounds like the site owner. Use for any new or changed text on a client site: product descriptions, category and guide pages, blog posts, FAQ answers, landing pages. The project's own voice notes and facts live in its docs folder; this guide is the method.

## Before you write

1. Read the project's rules first: `CLIENT-SITE.md` and the project index, the voice notes (for example `docs/VOICE.md`) if the project has them, and the plan's writing rules. The project's rules win on conflict.
2. **Fact-check first.** Every factual claim (property, figure, process step, safety statement, comparison, date, price, availability, stock) comes from one of:
   - a page or document on the site, read in this session (product page, datasheet, safety sheet);
   - a source you read and can cite (manufacturer docs, a standard), with its URL noted in your working notes.
   If neither exists, do not write the claim. Leave it out, or ask.
3. Never guess, estimate, round or fill a gap with a plausible value. A missing figure is reported, not invented. When two sources disagree, the owner's own product page wins, and the disagreement is reported.
4. Read the existing pages you will link to or compare, so the new text matches the site's terms and product names.

## Voice

- Name the site's own speaker (we, the shop) and the reader's form of address; use the one the site already uses.
- Warm, direct, practical. Write for the reader's situation, one step at a time.
- Specific over grand: the number, the product, the use.
- Plain language. Short and medium sentences. Define a term once when a newcomer would not know it.
- Honest about limits: say which product fits which job, and when another one is better.

## Format

- Guides and blog posts: a short intro that names the problem, numbered sections (question headings where they fit), a short closing line with a next step. No long summary at the end.
- Product pages: a one-line purpose, a "uses" list, a "specifications" list with units, and the mix and time facts as short lines.
- Units consistent with the site. Ratios and product names exactly as in the catalogue.
- Comparison tables: one row per property, the same property order for every product.

## Forbidden

- Em dashes and en dashes in new text. Use a comma, a colon, a period, or a word such as "to". (Old text on the site may still have them; do not copy them.)
- Emoji, decorative symbols, hidden or zero-width characters, decorative arrows and check marks.
- Inflated filler: grand adjectives that add nothing ("unique", "unmatched", "inspiring", "perfect"), promises of results.
- Invented facts: durability, safety, results or guarantees the source does not state.
- Machine patterns: a rhetorical question in every paragraph; three-part praise lists; stock closers ("in conclusion"); "it is important to know"; filler reassurance; a closing line that repeats the intro.

## Human-writing check (before you hand over)

1. Does each paragraph add one fact, step or reason? Cut the rest.
2. **Source check:** every claim traces to a line in a page, a document or a cited source. Keep the source list in working notes, not in the copy. Remove anything without one.
3. Figures match the product page exactly, with units, and match every other mention of that product.
4. No forbidden symbol or dash.
5. Would the intended reader recognise their situation in the first two sentences?
6. Is each claim something the owner can stand behind?

Do not aim for an AI-detector score. The aim is clear, accurate writing that the owner would sign.

## Output

- Return only the text, in the form the page needs (headings, lists, table), with no commentary.
- Missing figures or unclear products: say so in the reply, outside the text. Do not put brackets in the copy.
- Translations: do not write them by hand. Use the site's translation setup, if it has one, and check the result.
